Jazzdor Festival Microsite

A digital identity and microsite for a contemporary jazz festival.

Timeline
2 Weeks, 2025
Team
Faith Leung | Ysan Casis
Brooke Zyrmiak | Rebecca Yeung

Project Overview

Jazzdor is a contemporary jazz festival known for its bold visual identity and improvisational spirit. As part of a five-person team, I helped translate that identity into a usable, accessible digital experience, focusing on UX structure, research, and interaction clarity so visitors could quickly find artists, locations, showtimes, and tickets.

Problem
Balancing a bold, expressive visual identity with clear information hierarchy and accessible interaction patterns for a diverse audience.

Outcome
A visually expressive yet structured microsite that keeps Jazzdor's energy intact while staying clear, accessible, and easy to use across content-heavy pages.

My Role

I focused on UX structure, research, and interaction clarity, working to keep the bold visual identity from overwhelming usability.

  • Research: Studied audience behaviour and accessibility needs across festival microsites.
  • Information architecture: Mapped how users browse lineups, schedules, and tickets.
  • Interaction design: Built the navigation and hierarchy that keep bold visuals legible.
  • Accessibility: Checked readability and contrast across expressive layouts.

Research & Process

Rather than start with visuals, I looked at how people actually use festival websites, and where accessibility tends to break down in expressive designs.

The research showed:

  • Users often feel overwhelmed by dense layouts and experimental visuals.
  • Key information like showtimes and ticketing is frequently buried.
  • Expressive typography and motion can hurt readability without structure.
  • Users respond best to modular layouts with clear hierarchy.

These findings shaped the microsite's layout, navigation, and interaction design.

Design Scope

How might we express Jazzdor's bold visual identity while keeping the experience clear, accessible, and easy to navigate?

  • Express the identity: Translate Jazzdor's improvisational energy into digital form through bold typography, colour, and layout.
  • Maintain clarity: Let users quickly find artists, locations, showtimes, and tickets despite expressive visuals.
  • Support accessibility: Preserve readability through contrast, spacing, and type scale, and reduce visual overload with structured layouts.

Visual Identity

Our team built the microsite's full visual identity. A dark background lets individual artists stand out, and geometric line work adds dimension while guiding users through the experience. Bright, complementary colours mark focus points and active states.

Content-heavy pages shift toward a structured, functional layout, while expressive, collage-like compositions appear only where they won't compete with clarity.

Clash Display is a wide and round typeface, drawing attention and engulfing negative space. We implemented it in our headers and subheaders. The coolness of Clash Display pairs with the structured DM Mono, adding a timeless body and murmur to the site.

Colours

The site's complimentary colour palette shines against the black background. Images are desaturated to mimic moments frozen in time. When hovered, images are treated with an orange or blue overlay for indication.

Logotype / Type Treatment

Stemmed from our header, the Jazzdor logo replaces vowels with bright geometric shapes, mimicking the original letter. To take layering a step further, we add a series of smaller ellipses, outlining the original letter form and add noise.

User Flow

From the landing page, visitors move through a brief festival intro before branching to artists, cities, or tickets — with quick paths back into ticketing from every branch.

Sitemap

Eight core screens carry the experience from festival overview to individual artist and ticketing pages.

About the Festival

The first section after the landing page offers visitors information about what to expect from the Jazzdor Strasbourg Festival.

Featured Artists

The featured artists section highlights some of the performing artists with interactive photos that lead to their artist page.

City Finder

The location section provides information about the cities involved in the Strasbourg Festival, with a button to in-depth information about each city and its performances.

Tickets

The ticket page, located at the bottom of the home page, has three ticketing options users can click through to buy their tickets.

Full Artist List

The full artist list organizes artists by location of performance — users hover through and click an artist to learn more.

Individual Artist

The individual artist page scrolls horizontally, providing more information on the artist and their performance, and links to ticketing.

Locations

The location page provides information about the city performances take place in, plus a list of musicians playing there.

Showtimes

Nested under Locations, this horizontally-scrolling section guides users through musicians performing in the city, location, and time.
